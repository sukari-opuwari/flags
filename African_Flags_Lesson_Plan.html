African flags lesson plan

Age 4   |   25 minutes   |   Whole group and individual work

Children explore the flags of Nigeria, Ghana, Botswana and Somalia through looking, talking and a supported circle-the-flag activity. Use the accompanying African Flags Worksheet.

Learning aims

By the end, children will have opportunities to:

  • Match at least two spoken country names to their flags using the picture key and adult support.
  • Point to or name a colour, stripe or star on a flag.
  • Listen to one short instruction and respond by pointing or circling.

Country-name recall is emerging at this age; independent reading and memorising all four flags are not expected.

Prepare

  • Print worksheet page 1 in colour for each child. Keep page 2 as the adult answer guide.
  • Display a colour copy of the picture key. Cover the questions during the introduction.
  • Provide thick pencils or crayons. Have a globe or map showing Africa ready, if available.

Teach the lesson

1 Welcome and notice | 3 minutes

Show the four flags. Ask: “What colours can you see?” Accept pointing, gestures and spoken answers. Explain: “A flag can help us recognise a country. These flags belong to four countries in Africa. Africa is a continent with many countries.” Point to Africa on your map, if using one.

2 Meet the four flags | 5 minutes

Point to each flag, say its country name clearly and invite children to repeat it. Use one visual clue for each:

  • Nigeria: green, white, green stripes.
  • Ghana: red, yellow and green, with a black star.
  • Botswana: blue, with a black stripe edged in white.
  • Somalia: blue, with a white star.

Ask: “Which two flags have stars?” Help children point to Ghana and Somalia.

African flags lesson sequence

Continue with the same four flags and the worksheet picture key.

3 Play point to the flag | 4 minutes

Say: “Point to Nigeria.” Wait, then point together and repeat the name. Try the other countries. Mix country prompts with clues: “Find the white star” or “Find the green, white and green stripes.” Invite everyone to stretch their arms into a star shape before sitting for the worksheet.

4 Complete the worksheet | 8 minutes

Model the first question: “Find Nigeria. Let’s look at Nigeria in our picture key. Now look at these two flags. Which one matches?” Demonstrate circling the whole flag. This first answer is guided practice.

Read questions 2 to 4 aloud, one at a time. Keep the key visible and give children time to compare. Ask, “What do you notice?” before offering a clue. Accept pointing if circling is difficult; an adult can record the choice. Praise careful looking and effort.

Answer key: 1 Nigeria - right; 2 Ghana - left; 3 Botswana - right; 4 Somalia - left.

5 Share and check | 5 minutes

Ask: “Which flag has a black star?” (Ghana.) “Point to a flag with blue.” (Botswana or Somalia.) Invite children to choose a favourite flag and describe one feature.

For a quick individual check, say two country names in a different order and ask each child to point to their flags in the key. Record whether the child matched them independently, with a clue, or after a model. Use this alongside worksheet observations; the modelled first answer does not show independent recognition.

Adapt to the child

More support: Begin with Nigeria and Somalia only. Say and point to each name, then offer the two flags. Repeat slowly, allow extra response time and accept home-language colour words or gestures.

More challenge: Ask the child to explain a choice using a stripe or star. If ready, briefly cover the key and ask for one familiar country, then uncover it to check together.

Next time

Repeat the pointing game in a new order. Revisit two flags if needed before introducing more countries. Optional follow-up: children draw their own flag and describe its colours; explain that it is their own design.
